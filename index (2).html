Fragmented records

Connect transactions instead of editing balances by hand.

Business workflow

From Customer Request to Completed Sale

1Customer request
2Select dimensions
3Calculate volume
4Calculate price
5Confirm sale
6Record payment
7Update stock and records
  • The calculated amount (volume × price per m³) may differ from the final agreed amount after negotiation.
  • A sale can be fully paid, partly paid or on credit; unpaid amounts stay on the customer’s account.
  • Stock, customer balance and cash update according to the payment type, and an invoice can be printed.

Key decisions

Decisions That Shaped the System

Balances from ledger rows

DecisionBalances come from recorded movements, not typed totals.

WhyEvery figure traces back to the invoice or payment behind it.

Trade-offFixing a mistake means recording a correction, not overtyping.

Reversals, not deletions

DecisionCancel with a reversal entry and keep the original.

WhyA past invoice feeds balances, stock and cash, so overwriting it hides changes.

Trade-offMore rows to read; screens need a toggle for reversed entries.

Calculated vs agreed price

DecisionStore both amounts and report the difference as a discount.

WhyThe calculation stays objective while negotiation becomes measurable.

Trade-offTwo amounts per invoice and an alert threshold for unusual discounts.

Stock by timber size

DecisionEvery distinct size has its own stock card.

WhyThe real question is always “do we have this size?”

Trade-offMore items to maintain: every new non-standard length becomes a new size.

Testing and validation

Validating the System Against Real Business Scenarios

Test cases and acceptance criteria I defined, not a claim that each has been run. Separately, the owner compared migrated Excel balances with the system.

Cash sale
Invoice recorded; stock decreases; cash updates; customer balance shows completed payment.
Credit sale
Invoice recorded; stock decreases; unpaid amount recorded on the customer; cash not increased.
Partial payment
Payment recorded; remainder stays as debt; customer balance correct.
Purchase
Recorded against the supplier; stock increases; payment status reflected.
Cancellation
Original preserved; reversal entries recorded; stock and balances adjusted.
Volume and pricing
Volume matches dimensions and quantity; calculated and agreed amounts both stored.

Outcomes and next steps

What Changed & What I Learned

What changed

  • Fragmented spreadsheets became one integrated application
  • Sales, inventory and finance are connected
  • Transactions are traceable through reversals and an audit log
  • Structured data is ready for analytics

What I learned

  • AI-assisted development is only as good as the business clarity you give it
  • Acceptance criteria up front make each build phase easy to judge
  • Designing for traceability early prevents manual errors
